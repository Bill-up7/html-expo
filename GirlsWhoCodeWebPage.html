Girls Who Code at Red Mountain

Girls Who Code is a nonprofit organization founded in 2012 by Reshma Saujani. The organization was created to close the gender gap in the technology industry by encouraging and supporting girls and nonbinary students in computer science. Girls Who Code provides programs, clubs, and resources that teach coding skills while also building confidence, leadership, and problem-solving abilities. Its mission is to inspire students to pursue careers in technology and to create a more inclusive and diverse future in the tech world.

Working with robots:

During the first semester, we worked with robots that used a touch-and-go mechanism. The robots connected to a tablet via Bluetooth, and the tablet used magnets to represent binary code (zeros and ones). Although these robots were designed for K–5 students, we presented them to teachers so they could use them to teach their students how to code. Learn more about binary code here!!

We also learned about FIRST LEGO League, an international robotics program where students build, design, and program LEGO robots to complete challenges. FIRST LEGO League focuses on teamwork, problem-solving, and creativity while helping students learn coding and engineering skills through hands-on experience. Learn more about FIRST LEGO League

Girls Get IT

Girls Get IT was in November at Scottsdale Community College. Girls Get IT is an event where you listen to a guest speaker and split into different stations to learn about computer science. You also get to talk to women who are in the industry and ask them questions about their careers or what the industry is like.
